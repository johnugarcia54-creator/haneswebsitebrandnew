How it's made

Strength, crossed.

Plywood is made from thin veneers of timber, laid with the grain of each layer running across the one below, then bonded into one solid sheet. It's what keeps a sheet stable, stops it splitting and makes it strong in both directions.

  1. Peeled into veneers.

    Logs are peeled into thin, continuous sheets of veneer.

  2. Turned 90°, layer by layer.

    Every other veneer is laid across the grain of the one below it.

  3. Bonded into one sheet.

    The veneers are glued and pressed under heat and pressure into a single panel.

  4. Line them up, and it's LVL.

    Lay every veneer the same way and you get laminated veneer lumber: long, straight and strong along its length.

LVL

Beams you can build on.

Laminated veneer lumber lays every veneer along its length, so each beam comes out long, straight and consistent, from one length to the next. Made to AS/NZS 4357.1

Long and straight.

Veneers laminated along the length make LVL less prone to twist and bow than sawn timber.

Consistent.

Engineered from veneers, so one length performs like the next.

Made for spans.

For beams, lintels and headers across wide openings.
